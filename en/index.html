The event

A turning point in Todtnau's history

On 19 July 1876, a major fire destroyed much of Todtnau. The disaster shaped the town and its collective memory for generations. This website documents additional research, places reported figures in context and asks questions of the historical sources.

Overview

19 July 1876 – Timeline

According to fire brigade adjutant Hanser, the fire began around noon on 19 July 1876 at Ziegler's paper mill. By about 3 p.m., large parts of the town were burning; by about 5 p.m., the fire was under control.

Around noon

Hanser locates the outbreak in the upper rooms of Ziegler's paper mill. The available excerpt does not identify the actual cause of ignition.

During the afternoon

The south-westerly wind carried burning rags and pieces of paper into the town. According to Hanser, by 3 p.m. the buildings marked on his accompanying map were ablaze; the fire was not brought under control with outside help until around 5 p.m.

After the fire

Sources give differing damage figures: 88 houses in an early newspaper report, 89 residential houses and 61 outbuildings in the supplied material associated with Hanser, and 149 buildings in a later retrospective.

Following days

After the fire, Todtnau's fire brigade and brigades arriving from surrounding communities jointly maintained a fire watch for four days. At the same time, aid for homeless residents and the organisation of relief measures began.

The times follow Hanser's 1877 report; building damage figures differ by source.

How extensive were the losses?

The reported damage figures do not fully agree. Different accounts give different numbers and categories of buildings. Each figure is therefore presented together with its source.

88 houses

The Schwäbische Kronik of 22 July 1876 reported 88 houses, 202 families and approximately 1,200 homeless people.

89 residential houses and 61 outbuildings

The supplied text gives these figures in connection with Hanser's report: 150 buildings in total. The precise boundary between the eyewitness account and later additions must be considered.

A subsequent death

According to Hanser, nobody died during the fire itself. However, the wife of Mayer, landlord of the Sonne inn, died of her burns a few days later. Other people were also injured.

Sources give differing damage figures: 88 houses, 89 residential houses and 61 outbuildings, and 149 destroyed buildings in a 2025 retrospective. No definitive explanation of the differences has yet been documented.

What eyewitness Hanser reported

Adjutant Hanser of the Todtnau Volunteer Fire Brigade described the fire of 19 July 1876 in the Badische Feuerwehrzeitung in 1877. His account is among the most important testimonies to the disaster.

Noon: fire at the paper mill

Around noon, fire broke out in the upper rooms of Ziegler's paper mill. A south-westerly wind carried burning rags and paper into the town.

3 p.m.: several districts burning

The church, rectory, houses near the town hall and the upper town were affected. Hanser describes rapidly shifting seats of fire and buildings reigniting.

Around 5 p.m.: fire under control

With the support of numerous fire brigades from surrounding communities, the fire was fully brought under control at around 5 p.m., according to Hanser.

The sources behind the story

The course of the fire is documented particularly by fire brigade adjutant Hanser's report in the Badische Feuerwehrzeitung (1877). The Schwäbische Kronik, no. 172 of 22 July 1876, records the immediate aftermath. Parish records, archive files and later local accounts provide additional evidence of reconstruction.

Tribute · Todtnau local history

Special thanks to Benno Dörflinger

Research into the Todtnau fire builds on the work of people who have sought out, preserved and made historical evidence accessible. Special thanks go to Benno Dörflinger for his work on Todtnau's history and on preserving the memory of the fire of 1876.

His article “The Great Fire of Todtnau 150 Years Ago”, published in the Todtnauer Nachrichten on 17 July 2026, brings together historical reports and images and provides valuable starting points for further source verification.

This tribute acknowledges his historical work. The assessment and interpretation of individual sources on this website remain independent.
