Contemporary reports and their claims

  1. Record the original: Document the scan, newspaper title, date, page and archive reference.
  2. Check the wording: Transcribe the statement precisely and flag possible reading errors.
  3. Compare terminology: Do not equate “house”, “building”, “family” and “homeless”.
  4. Assess independence: Determine whether later texts copied from one another.
  5. Label the finding: Verified, probable or still unresolved.

Particularly important are the damage and reconstruction files of the Schönau District Office in the Freiburg State Archive (collection B 738/1). Their specific details will only be used after examination.

Archive records conflict – original sources still to be checked

When did the fire begin? Checking times in the original sources

HG-SBT-0002: The research record for Pastor Scherer's entry gives the start of the fire as around noon on 19 July 1876. The entry is described as a near-contemporary eyewitness account.

HG-SBT-Z-0005: The archive record for the Schwäbische Kronik (22 July 1876) gives 7 p.m. Whether this time actually appears in the original newspaper has not yet been confirmed.

Important: A difference between two archive descriptions is not yet a proven contradiction between historical sources. The 7 p.m. figure could also result from a cataloguing or reading error.

Next verification step: Compare images and transcriptions of both passages side by side; also determine whether one time refers to the outbreak and the other to a later stage.

References: Research archive HG-SBT-0002 (parish register) and HG-SBT-Z-0005 (Schwäbische Kronik, no. 172, 22 July 1876).

Unresolved

88, 89 and 149: differing damage figures

Our existing sources: 88 houses destroyed.

Later municipal account: 149 buildings.

Question to investigate: Were residential houses and all buildings counted differently? This is a possible explanation, not yet a finding. Contemporary damage lists and definitions of counting units are needed.

Unresolved

Three or four hours?

A summary in the research archive gives a period of about three hours. A municipal retrospective gives four hours. It remains to be established whether these refer to the same phase: the main fire, its spread or its containment.

Point of origin ≠ cause

What happened at the paper mill?

The mill is identified as the point of origin; the town history places the fire in the upper roof area. This does not explain what ignited it. No specific cause should be asserted without an investigation report.

Names to clarify

Emil or Josef Ziegler?

Existing accounts associate the mill with Emil Ziegler. A biographical entry names Josef Ziegler as the person who took it over in 1875. Ownership, management and company name must be examined separately; this does not yet establish a contradiction.

Estimate to verify

Around 1,000 homeless people?

This frequently cited number needs a contemporary reference and clarification of whether it counts those affected, people without shelter or residents receiving support.

Distinguishing weather evidence

Wind and drought

Regional weather observations may clarify the context. Measurements from Basel in June 1876 do not, however, establish wind direction or weather in Todtnau on 19 July. Claims about the day of the fire require sources matching the place and time.

New archival lead

Building administration before the fire

A related group of handwritten records from spring 1875 documents correspondence between Todtnau municipal officials and the Schönau District Office. It concerns local building administration and the bodies involved.

The documents show that building administration was already under official discussion before the fire. Whether and how these matters influenced reconstruction planning in 1876/77 remains unresolved.

Help from Wembach: a fire brigade deployment in July 1876

The Todtnau fire could not be fought by local forces alone. The Wembach firefighters also attended on 19 July 1876. According to the municipality's published fire brigade history, the fire engine first had to be harnessed and transported to Todtnau by horse. Under these conditions, immediate arrival was impossible.

A particularly vivid piece of evidence is the surviving catering invoice: it lists 13½ litres of wine, four loaves of bread and one kilogram of Swiss cheese for the day of the fire. Firefighting continued in Todtnau the following day. The invoice thus documents both the provision of food to helpers and firefighting beyond the first day.

Source: Municipality of Wembach, “History of the Wembach Fire Brigade”, Online account. The original invoice has not yet been independently examined.

Wind and flying embers

In its historical account, the Town of Todtnau describes a south-westerly wind carrying burning rags, pieces of paper and masses of paper from the mill area over the rooftops. The municipal account mentions a total of 23 pumps and assistance from several towns. These details are presented here as a later local history account; verification against the earliest fire reports remains necessary.

Source: Town of Todtnau, History.

Eyewitness account · 1877

Adjutant Hanser: From factory fire to town disaster

In his report published in 1877, fire brigade adjutant Hanser describes the outbreak at around noon in Ziegler's paper mill. Burning paper and rags were carried into town by the south-westerly wind; the dry wooden buildings and shingle roofs favoured rapid spread.

According to Hanser, by 3 p.m. the buildings marked on an accompanying map were burning. Around 5 p.m. the combined firefighting crews managed to bring the fire fully under control. Besides Todtnau, Hanser names numerous helpers from surrounding communities.

Hanser describes how Gendarme Laux rescued Mrs Montigel from the burning town hall. Laux later received a large silver medal of merit. Hanser mentions several injured people and the subsequent death of the wife of innkeeper Mayer of the Sonne as a result of burns.

Source note: Hanser, report in the Badische Feuerwehrzeitung (1877), preserved in archive document HG-SBT-0008. The following section of the supplied text, on reconstruction and the Grand Duke, must be distinguished as a later summary until its exact boundary has been verified against the original.

Building losses: The supplied text gives 89 residential houses and 61 outbuildings; the Schwäbische Kronik mentions 88 houses, while a later retrospective gives 149 buildings. The different counting methods have not been fully clarified.
