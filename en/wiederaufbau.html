A specific lead: the main road

The Freiburg State Archive holds a file concerning the alteration of the main road within Todtnau during reconstruction under reference: B 719/1 no. 1560, dating from 1876–1879 and comprising five sheets.

The file title alone shows that reconstruction also affected the road layout. Only an examination of the sheets can establish what measures were planned or actually carried out.

Context within Nessler research

Karl Ludwig Nessler was born in Todtnau in 1872 and was four years old at the time of the fire. The event therefore forms part of the historical setting of his early childhood. This does not establish any personal experience or recollection of the fire by Nessler.

Urban development

Reconstruction after 1876

The destruction made it necessary to reorganise the town centre. According to a retrospective in the Todtnauer Nachrichten of 25 July 2025, the ruins of the old church were demolished by blasting in January 1877. A new town plan was reportedly adopted on 22 February 1877.

The plan included wider streets and a more spacious market square. The changed layout was also intended to prevent fires from spreading rapidly in future. The official building and road records are essential for reconstructing the precise measures.

The church as a landmark

The historical drawing of the main street depicts the pre-1876 townscape with the old single-towered church. A photograph of the fire ruins documents the severe destruction. Later views show the new church with two towers. The images are not identical viewpoints and are not presented as an exact before-and-after comparison of individual buildings.

Reconstruction and aid: two specific archival leads

Archive finding aids identify important files on road alterations, donation collections and reconstruction. Their catalogue descriptions must be distinguished from the actual file contents.

Main road within Todtnau (1876–1879)

The Freiburg State Archive holds, under B 719/1 no. 1560 , a five-sheet file entitled Alteration of the main road within the town of Todtnau during reconstruction after the town fire of 1876. This officially documents that the road layout was the subject of an administrative procedure. The catalogue entry alone does not establish whether or how particular measures were implemented.

Finding aid: Baden-Württemberg State Archives / LEO-BW, B 719/1 no. 1560.

Donations and accountability: Stockach (1876–1877)

The inventory of the Stockach Town Archive lists, under II.2/2 , a file entitled Collection for the victims of the Todtnau fire. Accountability report of the relief committee (1876–1877). This reference broadens our search for relief provided beyond the region. The amount collected, the recipients and the exact contents of the report are not yet known.

Finding aid: Stockach Town Archive, collection II.2/2 (PDF).

A townscape shaped by reconstruction

The State Archives' historical gazetteer describes Todtnau's new layout as a grid pattern with a rectangular market square. The new two-towered parish church was built in 1878–1881 on a different site. These details help interpret our historical views, but do not establish the dates of individual images.

Reference: LEO-BW, Historical Gazetteer: Todtnau.

Newly identified archive reference

1877: A collection to rebuild the church

A finding aid for Baden municipal archives expressly lists, among the records of the town of Möhringen, a “Collection for the reconstruction of Todtnau church destroyed by fire (1877)”. This shows that support for the new church was recorded outside Todtnau. The catalogue entry does not establish the sum raised, the donors or the actual disbursement.

Source: Inventories of Baden Municipal Archives, Möhringen finding aid, “Fires and Fire Victims” section.

An earlier fire disaster: 1775

A file in the Karlsruhe General State Archive documents the restoration of the parish church destroyed by fire on 13 September 1775 and a contribution of 75 guilders from St Blasien Abbey. This concerns 1775, not the town fire of 1876. It offers a separate point of comparison for the repeated destruction and renewal of church buildings in Todtnau.

Source: Karlsruhe General State Archive, 229 no. 106227.

Source criticism: Both references have so far been obtained from finding aids. The original files have not yet been examined. In particular, the amount raised for the church rebuilding in 1877 remains unknown.
Aid and reconstruction

Relief supplies, the Grand Duke and Friedrichstrasse

According to the surviving account, food, clothing and monetary donations reached Todtnau after the disaster from Baden, other parts of Germany, Switzerland, Alsace, the Netherlands and England. Freiburg supplied market stalls as temporary accommodation.

The subsequent historical retrospective states that Grand Duke Frederick I visited Todtnau after the fire and made donations in goods and money. The new main street was named “Friedrichstrasse” in his honour. The text does not give an exact date for the visit.

According to the supplied account, a new town plan existed by February 1877 and reconstruction began in April. At times as many as 700 workers from outside the town, including many Italians, worked in Todtnau.

Source note: Summary section in the supplied text relating to HG-SBT-0008. Its boundary with Hanser's eyewitness account published in 1877 must be checked against the original.
